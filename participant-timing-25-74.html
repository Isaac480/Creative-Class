Multi-minute gaps inside the main rating task

Blur/focus only catches someone switching windows or tabs. Looking at per-trial time within the main face-rating phase turns up something the idle metric above misses entirely: individual slider-rating trials that took over a minute, with no accompanying blur event — meaning the tab stayed in focus the whole time.

7 of 50 (14%) have at least one face-rating trial over a minute. Participant 66 is the clearest case — 12.6 minutes of their 22.5-minute session is two individual slider trials, with only 16.4s of registered idle time anywhere in the whole session. That's a real gap in the idle-detection method: it only catches someone who switches windows, not someone who stays on the tab and pauses (distraction, interruption, or just a genuinely hard rating decision). Not a data-quality problem by itself — 66's reliability is 0.47, unremarkable, not excluded — but worth knowing before treating "0 idle time" as proof nothing happened.

What stands out

Participant 32 (already excluded for reliability failure) was also the fastest or near-fastest on every single survey — Creative Perceptions 11.8s, Work Habits 20.0s, KAI 33.4s, Big Five 10.0s, Three Good Lives 11.1s, Demographics 45.0s — all at or near this batch's minimum for that survey. Their reliability was -0.06 (already flagged by the pre-registered exclusion rule). Consistent story: this looks like someone clicking through without reading, not a fluke correlation.
Participant 52 (excluded for straight-lining) was not fast — their survey times are mid-to-slow (Demographics 237.9s, KAI 120.5s), not rushed. Worth noting because it's the opposite pattern from participant 32: straight-lining here means answering everything the same regardless of how much time was spent, not speeding through. Time-on-task alone wouldn't have caught this one.
Participant 68's Work Habits page (456.3s / 7.6 min) is the single longest survey time in the whole batch — nearly 30% longer than the next slowest (participant 73, 384.7s, also Work Habits). Both wrote substantive job descriptions (240 and 159 characters respectively), so this is plausibly genuine writing/thinking time rather than idle — Work Habits also has the free-text job-description field, same pattern flagged for participant 23 in the prior batch.
Participant 65's Demographics page took 490s (8.2 min) with zero registered idle time — but their actual answers are short and simple (no long free text; "comments" field empty, "issues" field is "N/A"). There's no blur/focus event around it, so this looks like the same blind spot as the mid-task gaps above: someone stepped away without switching windows or tabs.
KAI is still the slowest "quick" survey for most people (median ~100s across the batch, same pattern as the 7-10/20-24 batch), consistent with it being the longest instrument (33 items). Big Five and Three Good Lives remain the fastest for nearly everyone.

Method

Same reusable script as the prior report, unchanged except it now also tracks per-trial gaps within a phase (used for the mid-task section above) and is scoped to participants 25–74:

import ast
import csv
import sys

csv.field_size_limit(sys.maxsize)

def analyze_participant(row):
    trials = ast.literal_eval(row["json_data"])
    total_ms = trials[-1]["time_elapsed"]

    events = sorted(
        (be for t in trials for be in (t.get("browser_events") or [])),
        key=lambda e: e["time"],
    )
    idle_ms, blur_start = 0, None
    for e in events:
        if e["event"] == "blur":
            blur_start = e["time"]
        elif e["event"] == "focus" and blur_start is not None:
            idle_ms += e["time"] - blur_start
            blur_start = None

    phase_ms, last_phase, prev_t = {}, None, 0
    for t in trials:
        phase = t.get("experiment_phase") or last_phase
        t_elapsed = t.get("time_elapsed", prev_t)
        if phase:
            phase_ms[phase] = phase_ms.get(phase, 0) + (t_elapsed - prev_t)
        prev_t, last_phase = t_elapsed, phase

    survey_rt = {
        t["form_name"]: t["rt"]
        for t in trials
        if t.get("experiment_phase") == "survey" and t.get("trial_type") == "render-mustache-template"
    }

    return {"id": row["id"], "total_ms": total_ms, "idle_ms": idle_ms,
            "phase_ms": phase_ms, "survey_rt_ms": survey_rt}

with open("data/temp/data.csv") as f:
    for row in csv.DictReader(f):
        if row.get("worker_id"):
            print(analyze_participant(row))

Same two caveats as before still apply, and the mid-task gap section above is a direct illustration of the second one: idle detection only catches tab switches, and this batch shows real cases of it missing genuine walk-aways that stayed on-tab.
