Short answer

H1 is supported in this batch: the pre-registered FaceAge × CreativePerceptions interaction is negative and significant (b = -0.197, p < .0001) — participants who perceive a stronger rise of the creative class show a weaker positive relationship between a face's age and their willingness to seek professional advice from that face, exactly as predicted. H2 (domain specificity) still can't be tested — every analyzed participant is in the professional-advice condition; there's no emotional- or intelligence-condition data yet. The two other secondary IVs (Work Habits, KAI) don't show the effect; the composite Survey Score is in the right direction but not significant on its own.

H1 — primary confirmatory test SUPPORTED

Pre-registered model: Rating ~ FaceAge × CreativePerceptions + Big5 + FaceSex + (1 + FaceAge | Participant) + (1 | Face), fit within the professional-advice condition. Fell back to no_slope (dropped the random slope entirely) in the convergence ladder.

What that means concretely: at low Creative Perceptions (~2 on the survey's scale), each extra year of estimated face age is worth about +0.71 points of professional-advice-seeking rating. At high Creative Perceptions (~4.4, roughly this batch's average), that same year is worth only about +0.23 points — the age-advice link is real but nearly a third as strong for people who see the creative class as ascendant. That's the predicted pathway: perceived rise of the creative class → weaker preference for older-looking faces as professional advisors.

None of the five Big Five traits or FaceSex reach significance as controls (all p > .37) — the interaction isn't being driven by a personality confound that happens to correlate with Creative Perceptions.

H2 — domain specificity NOT TESTABLE

Pre-registered model: Rating ~ Condition × FaceAge × CreativePerceptions + FaceSex + (...), professional + emotional conditions pooled. Not in this results file — same situation as the 9-pilot batch: everyone who actually completed the study in this range is in the professional condition. (Three participant rows in this ID range carry a garbled condition value and status: timeout with no submitted data — not real emotional/intelligence condition data, just abandoned sessions; worth a look at whatever assigns/writes the condition field if that recurs.) The mechanism-probe model (intelligence condition) is in the same boat — can't be fit until that condition has completers.

Of the three constituents of "creative-class alignment," only Creative Perceptions itself (the primary IV) shows the effect. Work Habits (how creative-class-aligned the participant's own job is) and KAI (personal adaptiveness/innovativeness) don't — at this sample size, at least. The composite blends all three, so a real CreativePerceptions effect gets diluted by two null components, which is consistent with why the composite lands short of significance despite pointing the same direction. Notably, the KAI model is the only one that converged with the full pre-registered random-effects structure — no fallback needed.

Exploratory model

Adds PsychRichness and demographics (age, sex, education, SES, political orientation) as exploratory main effects. The core FaceAge:CreativePerceptions estimate replicates almost exactly (-0.1968, p < .0001) — expected, since it's the same 47 participants with extra covariates bolted on, not new data.

One exploratory term reaches p < .05: PoliticalEconomic = "Somewhat conservative" (b = -29.09, p = .012, relative to the reference category). Treat this as noise, not a finding — see the caveat below on why.

How much to trust this

This is a real, if small, positive signal — not just "pipeline sanity check" territory. Unlike the 9-participant pilot (which existed purely to confirm the code ran correctly), this batch's key p-value (4.3 × 10⁻⁵) is small enough that it's unlikely to be pure noise, and it landed in the pre-registered direction. That said, N = 47 is still ~8% of the planned 600, all from one condition, and it's the first batch where this test was even meaningfully powered — one significant result at this stage is encouraging, not proof.
The exploratory model's p = .012 hit is fragile by construction. That model spends fixed-effect parameters on ~30 participant-level terms (5 Big Five + PsychRichness + age + sex + 6 education levels + SES + 6 economic-orientation levels + 6 social-orientation levels) with only 47 participants to estimate them from — which is why its Satterthwaite df for participant-level terms drops to ~17, versus ~40 in the other models. With that little residual information per parameter and no correction for the ~25 terms tested, a single p = .012 is exactly the kind of result you'd expect to see by chance and that could easily flip sign with 10 more participants. Worth watching as the sample grows, not worth acting on now.
3 of 50 excluded (participants 32, 52, 54) — same pre-registered rule as before (reliability ≤ 0 for 32/54, straight-lining for 52). None of the three would have changed the direction of the primary result; they're already out of the N = 47 used above.
