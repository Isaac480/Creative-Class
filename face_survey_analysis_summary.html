What changed

The Rmd previously ran an ad hoc pipeline (filter to 4 participants, correlate survey scores against the age of each person's Q1/median/Q3-rated face) that predates Isaac pre-registration draft.txt. It's now rebuilt to actually implement that pre-registration: exclusion criteria, TIPI Big Five scoring, the composite Survey Score, and the pre-registered lme4 mixed-effects models — run on all 9 real participants collected so far. The old quartile-face-age correlation section and its two plots are removed; they weren't part of the actual pre-registered plan and are superseded by the trial-level mixed models.

Two scoring bugs found and fixed

Work Habits reverse-scoring. The old pipeline reverse-scored Q3, Q9, and Q12 — but reading the actual item wording, those are all positively-phrased items ("solving problems with no obvious answer," "independence and freedom," "unique ideas or solutions"). The one genuinely reverse-worded item, Q6 ("the tasks on the job are simple and uncomplicated"), wasn't being reversed at all. This also matches the reverse item in the Work Design Questionnaire this survey is adapted from. Fixed to reverse Q6 only — you confirmed this while I was planning the rewrite.
Big Five and Three Good Lives were never being converted to numbers. Both surveys store responses as text like "Somewhat Agree (5)", not plain numbers. The old pipeline read them as-is, so any Big Five or psychological-richness computation would have silently produced garbage (or, as now, thrown an error, which is how this was caught). Both are now parsed via the trailing (N) before averaging. Spot-checked participant 20's Big Five scores and Work Habits score by hand against their raw responses — both match the pipeline's output exactly.

Exclusions

0 of 9 excluded. Reliability ranges 0.65-0.93 (well above both the r≤0 and the stricter r<.30 sensitivity cutoff), nobody self-reported prior participation, seriousness/attentiveness ranges 70-100 (all above the 50 cutoff), and the highest straight-lining share was 53% (well under the 90% cutoff). The bot-check/attention-check criterion can't be evaluated from stored data at all — both checks are validated client-side and block submission on failure, so every stored participant already passed by construction.

Reliability + how much participants agree with each other

Within-participant test-retest reliability (Step 3 in the Rmd) is written back out to its own file, exported_data/within_participant_reliability.csv — same numbers as in the exclusions table above, just on their own for easy reference.

To see cross-participant agreement directly, every participant's all-70 ratings are plotted on one chart, one row per face (sorted by that face's mean rating), colored by participant, with a black tick marking the mean:

8 of 9 participants track each other reasonably well — for most faces their ratings cluster loosely around the same region, moving up and down together as the sorted faces get more/less favorably rated overall. But participant 22 (light blue) stands apart: their ratings sit near 0 for the large majority of faces regardless of what everyone else says about that face — median rating 0, mean ~12 (vs. the rest of the group visibly spread 50-90 across the same faces). That's consistent with what the straight-lining check already flagged as this batch's highest share (53% of trials rated identically) without crossing the 90% exclusion cutoff — so it passes the pre-registered exclusion rule as written, but it's worth a look at participant 22's raw responses/comments before treating their data as equivalent to the other 8.

What still can't be tested

All 9 pilot participants are in the professional advice condition — there's no emotional or intelligence-condition data in the database yet. The domain-specificity test (Condition × FaceAge × CreativePerceptions) and the mechanism-probe model are documented in the Rmd with their pre-registered formulas, but not fit — nothing to contrast against yet. PsychRichness and the political/education/SES exploratory covariates only exist for participants 20-24 (added to the survey after 7-10 took part), so the exploratory model effectively runs on n=5.
